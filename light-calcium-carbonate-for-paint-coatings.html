Light Calcium Carbonate for Paint & Coatings: A Sourcing Guide

Precipitated calcium carbonate (PCC), also called light calcium carbonate, is one of the most widely used functional fillers in the paint and coatings industry. For paint manufacturers, the right PCC grade improves hiding power, controls gloss, reduces formulation cost, and keeps production stable. This guide explains what coatings buyers should look for, how PCC performs inside paint formulations, and how to source a consistent factory-direct supply.

What Is Light Calcium Carbonate (PCC)?

Light calcium carbonate is produced by a chemical precipitation process rather than by grinding natural limestone. The result is a fine, white, high-purity powder with controlled particle size and low impurity levels. In coatings, PCC is valued for its small, uniform particles, high whiteness, and consistent composition—properties that ground (heavy) calcium carbonate cannot reliably match.

For paint use, common grades range from 800 to 1500 mesh. A 1250-mesh PCC with a D97 particle size around 10 microns is a proven workhorse for interior and exterior architectural coatings, primers, and industrial finishes.

Key Specifications That Matter for Coatings

When sourcing light calcium carbonate for paint, focus on five numbers:

  • Whiteness: ≥94° improves hiding power and lets you reduce expensive titanium dioxide (TiO₂) loading.
  • Mesh / fineness: 1250 mesh (D97 ≈ 10.55 μm) gives smooth film appearance and low coarse-particle defects.
  • Oil absorption: around 60 ml/100g for standard PCC; lower oil absorption means less binder is consumed and viscosity stays manageable.
  • Moisture: ≤0.4% prevents lumping in the let-down stage and keeps viscosity stable.
  • pH: ~9.6, mildly alkaline, compatible with most water-borne and solvent-borne systems.

If your system needs better hydrophobicity and dispersion, ask for surface-treated (activated) PCC modified with stearic acid. Our SFA-PCC-99, with activation ≥99%, wets out faster in non-polar binders and reduces moisture pickup versus untreated PCC.

Why Paint Makers Use PCC

Coatings producers add light calcium carbonate for three reasons: cost control, optical performance, and process stability.

First, PCC is far cheaper than TiO₂ and resin, so it lets you extend the formula without a large sacrifice in hiding or durability. Second, its fine, regular particles improve gloss control, reduce sagging, and give a smoother topcoat. Third, consistent particle size keeps viscosity and tinting strength stable from batch to batch, which matters for automated filling lines.

Typical applications include interior and exterior wall paints, primers and undercoats, putty and filler, industrial enamels, powder coatings, and road marking paints. In each case, consistent whiteness and fineness are what keep the production line predictable.

How to Choose a PCC Supplier

Not all PCC is equal. When you evaluate a supplier, check four things:

  1. Grade consistency—request a recent COA and confirm the same specs ship every lot.
  2. Fineness control—verify D97 and particle distribution, not just a nominal mesh label.
  3. Documentation—COA, MSDS, and TDS should be available on request, plus third-party SGS if needed.
  4. Logistics—confirm FOB port, MOQ (usually 1×20ft container), lead time, and payment terms.

Factory-direct sourcing removes one layer of trading margin and gives you a direct line to the production team when you need a custom surface treatment or a spec adjustment for a specific paint system.
