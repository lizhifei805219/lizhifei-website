Light Calcium Carbonate for Masterbatch: A Sourcing Guide

Light calcium carbonate, also called precipitated calcium carbonate (PCC), is the backbone filler of the masterbatch industry. For masterbatch producers, the right PCC grade determines how much filler you can load, how smoothly the compound processes, and how clean the final pellet looks. This guide covers what masterbatch buyers should specify, how PCC behaves inside filler masterbatch, and how to secure a stable factory-direct supply.

What Is Light Calcium Carbonate (PCC)?

Light calcium carbonate is made by a chemical precipitation process rather than by grinding limestone. The result is a fine, white, high-purity powder with tightly controlled particle size and very low impurity. In masterbatch, PCC is preferred over ground (heavy) calcium carbonate because its smaller, more uniform particles disperse better in the carrier resin and produce fewer black specks and surface defects.

For filler masterbatch, the workhorse grade is typically 1250 mesh, where the D97 particle size is around 10 microns. This fineness lets you push high filler loading without wrecking the melt flow or the pellet appearance.

Key Specifications That Matter for Masterbatch

When sourcing light calcium carbonate for masterbatch, watch five numbers:

  • Mesh / particle size: 1250 mesh (D97 ≈ 10.55 μm) is the proven grade for high-loading filler masterbatch.
  • Whiteness: ≥94° keeps the masterbatch bright and avoids tinting the carrier resin.
  • Oil absorption: around 60 ml/100g for standard PCC; lower oil absorption means you can pack in more filler before viscosity spikes.
  • Moisture: ≤0.4% prevents bubbling and pellet rupture during extrusion.
  • pH: ~9.6, mildly alkaline, compatible with polyolefin carriers.

When dispersion or hydrophobicity is critical, ask for surface-treated (activated) PCC modified with stearic acid. Our SFA-PCC-99 grade, with activation ≥99%, couples better with non-polar polyolefin and lowers mix viscosity at high loadings.

Why Masterbatch Makers Use PCC

Filler masterbatch producers add light calcium carbonate for three reasons: cost, loadability, and process stability.

First, PCC is far cheaper than resin, so it lets you extend expensive polyolefin at 70–85% filler loading while keeping the pellet usable. Second, its fine, regular particles let you reach high loading without destroying melt strength or creating agglomerates. Third, consistent particle size keeps every production batch running at the same throughput.

Typical uses include PE/PP filler masterbatch, non-woven masterbatch, blown film masterbatch, injection molding masterbatch, and cable filling compounds. In each case, particle consistency is what keeps the extruder stable.

How to Choose a PCC Supplier

Not all PCC suits masterbatch. When you evaluate a supplier, check four things:

  1. Grade consistency—request a recent COA and confirm the same specs ship every lot.
  2. Mesh control—verify D97 and particle distribution, not just a nominal mesh label.
  3. Documentation—COA, MSDS, and TDS should be available on request, plus third-party SGS if needed.
  4. Logistics—confirm FOB port, MOQ (usually 1×20ft container), lead time, and payment terms.

Factory-direct sourcing removes one layer of trading margin and gives you a direct line to the production team when you need a custom surface treatment or a spec tweak for a new carrier resin.
