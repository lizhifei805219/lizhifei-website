Light Calcium Carbonate for Sealants & Adhesives

Light calcium carbonate, also called precipitated calcium carbonate (PCC), is a standard functional filler in silicone sealants and adhesives. For sealant and adhesive producers, the right PCC grade controls rheology, thixotropy, and cost without sacrificing bond strength. This guide covers what to specify for silicone sealant and adhesive formulations, how PCC behaves inside them, and how to lock in a stable factory-direct supply.

What Is Light Calcium Carbonate (PCC)?

Light calcium carbonate is produced by a chemical precipitation process rather than by grinding limestone. The result is a fine, white, high-purity powder with controlled particle size and very low impurity. In sealants and adhesives, PCC is valued because its small, uniform particles build viscosity predictably and stay stable in the cured film—properties that coarse ground (heavy) calcium carbonate cannot match.

For sealant and adhesive use, a fine grade such as 1250 mesh, where the D97 particle size is around 10 microns, gives the best balance of thixotropy and smoothness. Finer grades reduce surface texture; surface-treated grades improve compatibility with the polymer matrix.

Key Specifications That Matter

When sourcing light calcium carbonate for sealants and adhesives, focus on five numbers:

  • Mesh / particle size: 1250 mesh (D97 ≈ 10.55 μm) is a proven grade for silicone and polyurethane systems.
  • Whiteness: ≥94° keeps the cured bead bright and avoids yellowing in clear or white sealants.
  • Oil absorption: around 60 ml/100g for standard PCC; lower oil absorption gives more formulating freedom.
  • Moisture: ≤0.4% prevents bubbling and skinning defects during cure.
  • pH: ~9.6, mildly alkaline, compatible with most sealant chemistries.

For demanding adhesion or hydrophobic needs, use surface-treated (activated) PCC modified with stearic acid. Our SFA-PCC-99 grade, with activation ≥99%, couples better with the polymer and improves dispersion at high loading.

Why Sealant & Adhesive Makers Use PCC

Formulators add light calcium carbonate for three reasons: rheology control, cost, and reinforcement.

First, PCC builds thixotropy so the sealant stays put on a vertical joint and the adhesive holds without sag. Second, it is far cheaper than polymer resin, letting you extend expensive base polymer while keeping the bead workable. Third, its fine, regular particles reinforce the cured film and improve dimensional stability without gritty feel.

Typical uses include silicone sealants, acrylic sealants, polyurethane adhesives, construction adhesives, MS polymer sealants, and tile adhesives. In each case, consistent particle size keeps the production line and the cure predictable.

How to Choose a PCC Supplier

Not all PCC suits sealants. When you evaluate a supplier, check four things:

  1. Grade consistency—request a recent COA and confirm the same specs ship every lot.
  2. Mesh control—verify D97 and particle distribution, not just a nominal mesh label.
  3. Documentation—COA, MSDS, and TDS should be available on request, plus third-party SGS if needed.
  4. Logistics—confirm FOB port, MOQ (usually 1×20ft container), lead time, and payment terms.

Factory-direct sourcing removes one layer of trading margin and gives you a direct line to the production team when you need a custom surface treatment or a spec adjustment for a new polymer system.
