Light Calcium Carbonate for Incense Sticks: A Sourcing Guide

Incense manufacturing is a large and steady industry across Vietnam, Indonesia, and other parts of Southeast Asia. One of the most common raw materials in incense stick and cone formulations is light calcium carbonate (precipitated calcium carbonate, PCC). Used as a functional filler, it reduces formulation cost, improves combustion behavior, and keeps the finished incense uniform batch to batch. This guide explains what incense makers should look for when sourcing PCC, how it behaves in incense formulations, and how to secure a stable factory-direct supply.

What Is Light Calcium Carbonate (PCC)?

Light calcium carbonate is produced by a chemical precipitation process rather than by grinding natural limestone. The result is a fine, white, high-purity powder with controlled particle size and very low impurity levels. For incense use, this matters: a clean, consistent powder blends evenly with the fragrance and binder, leaves little residue, and does not affect the color or burn of the finished stick.

Typical incense grades are fine powders in the 600–1250 mesh range. For most stick and cone incense, a 1250-mesh PCC with a D97 particle size around 10 microns gives smooth mixing, good adhesion to the bamboo core, and a clean ash.

Key Specifications That Matter for Incense

When sourcing light calcium carbonate for incense, focus on four numbers:

  • Whiteness: ≥94° keeps the incense paste bright and the burned ash clean and light-colored.
  • Mesh / fineness: 1250 mesh (D97 ≈ 10.55 μm) is a proven grade for incense filler and paste; finer powder disperses better in the slurry.
  • Moisture: ≤0.4% prevents clumping during storage and uneven drying of the stick.
  • Purity (CaCO₃): ≥98% means fewer dark impurities and a more consistent burn.

If your formulation uses hydrophobic binders or you need better oil absorption control, ask about surface-treated (activated) PCC. Our SFA-PCC-99, stearic-acid modified with activation ≥99%, improves dispersion in oily fragrance blends and lowers moisture pickup versus untreated PCC.

Why Incense Makers Use PCC

Incense producers add light calcium carbonate for three practical reasons: cost control, process stability, and product quality.

First, PCC is far cheaper than the fragrance and binder components, so it lets you extend the formulation without a large sacrifice in aroma or burn quality. Second, its fine, regular particles improve the homogeneity of the incense paste, reducing cracks and breakage during drying. Third, consistent whiteness and low impurity keep the ash color uniform, which matters for premium incense brands.

Common uses include joss sticks, agarwood and sandalwood blends, cone incense, coiled mosquito and ritual incense, and powdered incense for temples. In each case, consistent particle size is what keeps the production line stable and the product uniform.

How to Choose a PCC Supplier

Not all PCC is equal. When you evaluate a supplier, check four things:

  1. Grade consistency—request a recent COA and confirm the same specs ship every lot.
  2. Fineness control—verify D97 and particle distribution, not just a nominal mesh label.
  3. Documentation—COA, MSDS, and TDS should be available on request, plus third-party SGS if needed.
  4. Logistics—confirm FOB port, MOQ (usually 1×20ft container), lead time, and payment terms.

Factory-direct sourcing removes one layer of trading margin and gives you a direct line to the production team when you need a custom surface treatment or a spec adjustment for a specific incense recipe.
